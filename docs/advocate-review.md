# Asking an advocate to review Spare Key

The judges' biggest remaining question is whether people who do this work think Spare Key helps. One advocate's
written review, quoted with permission, answers it. Nothing here needs a real survivor: the review uses the made-up
practice person, Jordan.

## Who to ask (pick two or three)

- Safety Net Project, National Network to End Domestic Violence: https://www.techsafety.org (contact form on the site)
- Clinic to End Tech Abuse, Cornell: https://ceta.tech.cornell.edu
- The NYU Tandon researchers behind the chatbot study the site cites (the study's news page lists the team)
- Your state's domestic violence coalition (list at https://ncadv.org/state-coalitions), or a local program's
  technology or legal advocate

## The message (edit the brackets, then send from your own email)

Subject: 10-minute review of a free, read-only tool for tech-enabled abuse?

Hello [name],

I built Spare Key for a hackathon: a free Chrome extension that reads a person's own Google, Gmail and Amazon
settings pages, read-only, and lists who can still see their location, mail or account. It then gives a plan that
starts with screenshots and an advocate, and warns before any change the other person may notice. Names and
addresses never leave the computer.

Would you look at it for about 10 minutes and tell me what is wrong or missing? Everything can be tried on made-up
pages, with no account and nothing to install:

- The practice check: https://spare-key-app.web.app/try.html
- What the other person sees when each kind of access is removed: https://spare-key-app.web.app/what-they-see.html
- The plan and how it is checked: https://spare-key-app.web.app/how-it-works.html

Five questions, short answers are perfect:
1. Would you show this to someone you work with? Why or why not?
2. Is anything in the plan unsafe, or in the wrong order?
3. Is anything in "what they see" wrong?
4. What is the most important thing it misses?
5. May I quote your answers, with your name and role, or anonymously as "a domestic violence advocate"? Or not at all?

Spare Key never says a person is safe, and it is not a replacement for an advocate: every plan tells the person to
talk to one, with the hotline (1-800-799-7233) on every screen.

Thank you for the work you do.
[Your name]
